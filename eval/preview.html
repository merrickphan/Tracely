School start times
Instrument Sans 15

American high schools are quietly running an experiment on adolescent brains. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommended in 2014 that middle and high schools start no earlier than 8:30 a.m., yet the majority of public high schools in the United States still begin before that. Teenagers require between eight and ten hours of sleep per night, and roughly 70 percent of them get less than seven. The consequence is not subtle: pushing start times later has been shown to increase how long students sleep, and districts that made the change saw fewer car crashes among teen drivers.

Studies show that this sleep debt is the primary driver of the adolescent mental health crisis, and experts agree that smartphones have made it worse. If schools do not act, depression rates among teenagers will double within the next decade. Yet districts keep citing bus schedules and after-school sports, which is a transparently weak excuse for compromising student health. Any school board that refuses to move its bell schedule is choosing logistical convenience over the wellbeing of the children it was elected to serve.

Remote work
Instrument Sans 15

The debate over remote work has been conducted almost entirely on vibes. A randomized controlled trial at a Chinese travel agency found that employees working from home were roughly 13 percent more productive than their in-office counterparts, and that attrition fell by half. That study is now cited constantly, usually by people who have not noticed that it studied call-center workers doing highly measurable, individual tasks.

Hybrid arrangements are different again. Research suggests that fully remote workers are less likely to be promoted than colleagues who come in, a gap sometimes attributed to reduced visibility rather than reduced output. Meanwhile, commercial office vacancy rates in major American cities reached record highs after 2020, which has put real pressure on municipal tax bases that depend on commercial property assessments.

The honest conclusion is that remote work is neither a productivity miracle nor a catastrophe. It is a reallocation of costs, and most of the loud claims on either side are extrapolating from a single study of a single firm. Companies mandating a return to the office are mostly responding to managerial anxiety, not evidence, and everyone involved would be better served by admitting how little we actually know.

The printing press
Instrument Sans 15

Johannes Gutenberg introduced movable-type printing to Europe around 1450, and within fifty years printing presses were operating in more than two hundred European cities. The conventional story treats this as the moment literacy and scientific progress became inevitable, but the causal chain is considerably messier than that.

Movable type had already existed in Korea and China for centuries before Gutenberg, which complicates any account that treats the technology itself as the decisive variable. What distinguished the European case was the alphabet's small character set, an existing paper industry, and a merchant class with money to spend on books. Cities that adopted printing early experienced faster economic growth in the following century, though disentangling that from the fact that wealthier cities could afford presses in the first place is genuinely difficult.
