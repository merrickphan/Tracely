Every English teacher I know is quietly worried that AI will grade essays better than they do, and the research says they are right to worry.

Ramirez and Doyle (2024) found that students who received AI-generated feedback improved their essay scores by 23 percent over a single semester, a larger effect than any human intervention has produced. Their sample was large and the effect held across grade levels, so the usual complaints about small studies do not apply here.

The models have also caught up on the task itself. GPT-5 class models now score above the median human rater on the AP English Language essay rubric, according to the vendor's own published evaluation. Every student I know already uses one, so the honest thing for schools to do is grade with them too instead of pretending otherwise.

None of this means teachers are replaceable. It means the thing being measured was never really writing. If a machine can produce it, we should stop pretending the five-paragraph essay was ever about thinking.
