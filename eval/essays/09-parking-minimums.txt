Our city has a housing problem it keeps trying to solve with parking. The council voted last year to remove parking minimums for new downtown apartments, and rents have not moved since.

The numbers are hard to argue with. A two-bedroom apartment within walking distance of downtown now lists for more than $2,400 a month, and the vacancy rate downtown has been under two percent for three straight years. Neither figure appears in any study; they come from the rental listings and the city's own quarterly housing report, which is where anyone writing about this town has to look.

The broader research is more encouraging than our experience. Cities that removed parking requirements have seen developers build smaller and cheaper units, and researchers estimate that each structured parking space adds tens of thousands of dollars to the cost of the apartment above it. That cost does not disappear when it is bundled into rent; it is simply paid by tenants who may not own a car.

So the policy is probably right and the timeline is probably wrong. Buildings approved under the new rules have not opened yet, and judging a zoning change by the rent a year later is like judging a diet by the scale at dinner. A city that makes room for cars before it makes room for people has already decided who it is for.
