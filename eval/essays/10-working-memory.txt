I take notes on a laptop in every class and I have started to think it is making me worse at the classes.

The reason is capacity. Working memory holds only about four items at a time, so a lecture delivered faster than a student can process it overruns the buffer and the rest is gone. Typing is fast enough to keep up with a lecturer almost word for word, which sounds like an advantage and is not. Transcribing a sentence does not require understanding it. Dividing attention during encoding reliably reduces how much of the material is recalled later. Handwriting is slow enough to force a choice about what matters, and making that choice is most of the studying.

My roommate disagrees, and her argument is the one everybody makes. Some students are visual learners and retain more when the same material is presented as diagrams, which is why a good lecturer draws. She types because the words are not what she is storing anyway.

None of this means laptops should be banned, and I would not give mine up. It means the note-taking app is not the neutral tool it looks like, and that the fastest way to write something down is not the same as the best way to keep it.
