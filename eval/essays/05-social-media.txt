Social media is the defining mental health crisis of our generation. Since smartphones became widespread around 2012, rates of anxiety and depression among teenagers have climbed steeply, and no other explanation fits the timing as well.

Orben and Przybylski (2019) analyzed data from over 350,000 adolescents and concluded that social media use is a primary driver of declining teen well-being. Their specification curve analysis is the most rigorous work anyone has done on the question, and it should settle the debate.

Some researchers argue the effect is small. But small average effects can hide large effects in vulnerable subgroups, and anyone who has watched a fourteen-year-old scroll for four hours straight knows what they are looking at.

Schools should ban phones outright. The evidence is already clear enough to act on, and waiting for perfect causal identification means waiting forever while a generation is harmed.
