I started washing dishes at my aunt's restaurant the summer I turned fifteen, and I have closed on Friday nights ever since.

A Friday is about a hundred and eighty covers. The dish pit runs four racks behind by seven o'clock and stays there until nine, and the walk home takes fourteen minutes, which is the only part of the night I get to myself.

I read somewhere that about a third of American teenagers work during the school year, which surprised me, because at my school it feels like nobody does.

Twenty-two hours a week is what I average, and the number matters more than it sounds like it should, because it is also four hours of homework I do not do. My chemistry grade went from an A to a B minus over one grading period and nobody in my family noticed, including me.

What I would say to anyone considering it is that the money is not the point and neither is the responsibility, whatever the counselors say about that. The point is that at eleven at night there are six of us left, and everything is quiet, and for an hour a week I am not the youngest person in the room.
