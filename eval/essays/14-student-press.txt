Our paper ran a story last spring about a teacher who left mid-year, and the principal asked us to pull it. We pulled it. I have spent most of this year trying to work out whether he was allowed to ask.

In Tinker v. Des Moines (1969) the Supreme Court held that students do not shed their constitutional rights to freedom of speech at the schoolhouse gate, and that a school may only censor speech it can show would materially disrupt the school.

Hazelwood v. Kuhlmeier narrowed that in 1988 by letting administrators exercise editorial control over a school-sponsored newspaper whenever they have a legitimate educational reason for doing so.

California is one of a minority of states that has written student journalists back out from under Hazelwood. Education Code section 48907 protects student expression unless it is obscene, libelous or slanderous, or incites students to break the law or substantially disrupt the school.

Because California has that statute, student newspapers here publish more investigative reporting than papers in states that rely on Hazelwood alone.

None of which tells me what to do next year, when the same thing happens and the adviser whose job depends on the principal is the person I have to ask.
