Why Sequence Models Took So Long

The architectures that now dominate machine translation and text generation were not obvious, and each one had to solve a problem the previous generation could not.

Recurrent networks were held back for years by a training failure that had nothing to do with data. Hochreiter and Schmidhuber [1] showed that a gated memory cell keeps the error signal from vanishing across long sequences, which made it possible to train recurrent networks on dependencies hundreds of steps apart.

The next shift abandoned recurrence altogether. Vaswani and Shazeer [3] found that an architecture built entirely from attention, with no recurrence and no convolution, reached higher translation quality than the recurrent models it replaced while training in a fraction of the time.

Environmental exposure research has moved in the opposite direction, toward smaller effects measured more carefully. Castellanos and Prewitt [2] found that microplastic concentrations in drinking water were associated with a 27 percent increase in circulating inflammatory markers across a cohort of 4,200 adults.
