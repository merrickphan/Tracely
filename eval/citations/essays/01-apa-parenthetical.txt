Sleep Loss and the School Day

Adolescents are not simply choosing to stay up late. Circadian rhythm shifts during puberty push sleep onset roughly two hours later, and this shift is biological rather than behavioural (Carskadon, 2011). Most American high schools still open before 8:00 a.m., which leaves the average student sleeping under seven hours on a school night.

The consequences are well documented. Wheaton and Ferro (2016) found that students at schools with later start times reported fewer depressive symptoms and better attendance across a two-year window. A separate analysis reached a similar conclusion using a national sample (Minges & Redeker, 2016). One district that moved its bell 50 minutes later recorded a 70% drop in teen car crashes ("Later School Start Times and Adolescent Crash Rates", 2018).

Not every finding points the same way. Some researchers argue that the effect sizes are modest once family income is controlled for (Dunster et al., 2018), and that is a fair objection to the strongest versions of the claim.

Cost is the usual objection raised at board meetings. Transportation budgets rise when bus routes cannot be staggered, though the estimates vary widely between districts (Jacob & Rockoff, 2011: 14). Even so, the balance of evidence favours a later start.

Schools should delay their opening bell to 8:30 a.m. or later. The evidence is not perfectly settled, but it points consistently in one direction, and the cost of being wrong is a decade of tired teenagers.
