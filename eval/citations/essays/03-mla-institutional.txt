Position Paper: Protection of Migrant Workers in Transit States

The delegation of Portugal recognises that migrant workers in transit states face abuses that existing instruments have not prevented. The Convention on the Rights of Migrant Workers has been ratified by fewer than a third of member states, and none of the major destination economies are among them ("Status of Ratification Interactive Dashboard").

Detention remains the default response in several transit corridors. Field reporting from Libya documents arbitrary detention of migrants intercepted at sea and returned to shore ("IOM Libya Migrant Report Round 44", 2022: 15). Conditions in these facilities have been described as incompatible with the minimum standards set out in international humanitarian law ("Report of the Independent Fact-Finding Mission on Libya").

Increased xenophobia has caused migrants to fear reporting abuses to any authority, which suppresses the very data states rely on to argue the problem is smaller than it is (Tyche Hendricks, 2024). Portugal notes that this dynamic makes prevalence estimates systematically low.

Remittances from migrant workers exceeded official development assistance by a factor of three in 2023 (World Bank, 2024). Any framework that treats migration solely as a security matter ignores the economic reality its own members depend on.

Portugal proposes a regional monitoring mechanism with reporting obligations modelled on the Convention's own Article 73 ("Background to the Convention"). The delegation looks forward to working with member states on language that both destination and origin countries can accept.
